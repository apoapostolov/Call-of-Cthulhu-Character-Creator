### Chapter 7: Shadow over Westhaven

This scenario is playable by up to six scoutinvestigators and may take several sessions T to complete. If part of the Westhaven Campaign, it works best as the conclusion.

The scout-investigators spend a weekend camping at Muskrat Lake. Soon, their pleasant adventure in New Hampshire’s Green Mountains turns to horror. First, when their scout leader is murdered, and second, when the scout-investigators return to Westhaven, they find it has undergone a dreadful transformation.

### Keeper Information

Jedediah Brumsted was a wealthy merchant and resident of Westhaven in the 19 [th] century. Brumsted devoted himself to the Windwalker, a Mythos deity of the frozen north. He needed a quiet place to worship, so he built a hunting lodge at Muskrat Lake, New Hampshire, some 100 miles (161 km) from Westhaven. The Windwalker promised great power, but Brumsted died before he could realize his dreams; he was buried at his lodge.

Brumsted’s descendants, Gustaf and Sydney, live on a farm south of town. The pair scratch out a living as best they can while serving as murderous acolytes of the Windwalker. They know little of Jedediah‘s story—whom they refer to as “grandfather,” though he’s really their great-greatgrandfather. They frequently use his old cabin for dark ceremonies and blood sacrifices.

Local cult leader, **Thomas Cline** (page 60), learned about Jedediah and convinced the siblings that he can unite their “grandfather” with the Windwalker; as Brumsted’s heirs, Gustaf and Sydney can inherit the Windwalker’s blessings. The first part is true: Cline is aiding Brumsted to ascend and join with the Windwalker; however, Cline will inherit the Windwalker’s blessing, not Brumsted’s descendants. Brumsted’s corpse has been dug up and moved to Westhaven in preparation for a magical ritual to summon the Windwalker.

Cline knows the scout-investigators may interfere, so he keeps the meddling kids away from Westhaven by orchestrating a camping trip to Muskrat Lake! Cline’s own daughter, Loretta, belongs to **The Stumbling Moose Squad** (page 65)—and he sends her to keep her out of harm’s way too. Cline is unaware that Muskrat Lake is haunted by monsters created by the murderous Sydney and Gustaf.

Scout leader **Paul Glenn** (page 56) eagerly accepted Cline’s offer of a weekend stay at a lakeside lodge. The trip consists of two overnight stays, during which Gustaf and Sydney act as lodge caretaker and cook.

In **Part One: Lakeside Horror** (page 136), two NPC (non-player character) scouts go missing on Saturday morning; the scout-investigators are sent to look for them. Later, when the scout leader discovers the bus sabotaged, tempers fray, and Paul Glenn is murdered. Scout-investigators must contend not only with the murderous Sydney and Gustaf, but also some terrifying, living wicker constructs.

Before they depart, scouts may search the lodge and discover Jedediah Brumsted’s now empty
grave in the cellar—clearly, they need to get back to Westhaven to stop worse horrors from happening!

In **Part Two: Hands of Winter** (page 150), the scout-investigators return to find Westhaven changed. Cline has the whole town under his spell: curious wooden towers are being constructed, while townsfolk hide in the shadows, and winter arrived early. Cline reanimates Jedediah Brumsted’s corpse in preparation for the scenario’s climax: the arrival of the Windwalker!

**Keeper note:** if relocating to Arkham, no major changes are needed for **Part One: Lakeside Horror** . In **Part Two: Hands of Winter** , adjust the duration of the Windwalker’s effect on the city (see **Adjusting for Arkham** , page 69).

### **Jedediah Brumsted,** _**age 143**_ **,** _**reanimated Cultist**_

Having first encountered the Windwalker when traversing the northern wilds, Brumsted spent much of his life seeking lore and exploring evil rituals. The Windwalker’s blessing granted him long life, but, eventually, he died and was buried before he could ascend to join his deity.

- **Description:** a horrifyingly withered, walking cadaver. His skin is hardened and leathery and stained a deep maroon-color. - **Roleplaying hooks:** Brumsted hates the living and does not hesitate to kill if his plans go awry.

### Leads: Obscure & Obvious Clues

At the end of key sections, we list “leads” to help Keepers run scenarios smoothly. This provides clear routes for scout-investigators to follow in terms of clues and plot progression. The leads tell Keepers which scene or event comes next, navigating the path the story can take from here.

- If the lead is labeled “obvious”—make scoutinvestigators aware of it as the scene progresses. They usually do not require a skill roll. Obvious leads are essential to the plot.
- If the lead is labeled “obscure”—the clue usually requires some form of specific action or a skill roll to access the information; or it may be available from a more easily accessible alternative source. Usually, obscure clues are not necessary to move the scenario forward.

### Arriving at Muskrat Lake

Muskrat Lake sits in a large, heavily forested depression surrounded by steep wooded hills on all sides. A wooden lodge sits facing a substantial clearing on the shore of the shining lake. Golden sunlight filters through a mix of oak and pine trees. Nearby, an exercise field features archery targets, a flagpole, and horseshoe pits.

Paul Glenn parks the bus and tells the scouts to gather in the dining room.

### The Lodge

The lodge comprises a single-story log cabin, 20 by 50 feet (6 by 15 m), with a wooden porch. The front door leads to a sizable dining room with two wooden tables and benches. A stone fireplace sits on the right, with a stuffed boar’s head projecting from the chimney. A painting of Muskrat Lake hangs on one wall. The front windows offer a captivating lake view, while the rear ones look out onto the dense forest behind.

A corridor leads to a trophy room and a library, with the kitchen and pantry beyond. The rustic kitchen has several counters, an assortment of pots and pans, a hand-pump water spigot, a wood-burning stove. The pantry has shelves of canned goods and jarred vegetables and a door opening onto a back porch.

Glenn then orders the troop to unload the bus. Gustaf directs them on where to stow provisions and gear. ### Dead of Night

It’s the middle of the night, and the scout-investigators are assumed to be asleep.

- Call for a **Dodge** roll for the scout concerned.
- **Failure:** the scout suffers 1 damage, with a cut to the head.
- A Hard **Spot Hidden** roll works out that Gustaf is holding an engine part.
- The resulting altercation wakes everyone.
- If a scout is injured, Gustaf insists he was halfasleep and lashed out in the dark, thinking it was an intruder—Glenn knows it’s a lame story, but has little choice but accept it for now.
- If scouts draw Glenn’s attention to the distributor cap, he demands to know why Gustaf removed it. Gustaf spins a yarn about wandering vagrants stealing vehicles at night, and how he removed the cap to stop such an occurrence.
- Glenn clearly doesn’t believe Gustaf but is at a loss for what to do. He orders everyone back to their tents, saying to Gustaf that they will talk about it in the morning.

### Wicker Ones

Gustaf and Sydney spawned these monsters in their midnight rituals at their **Woodland Altar** (page 143). A wicker one is created by wrapping a human corpse in interlacing branches and vines. The skin and heart are removed prior to wrapping, and the heart replaced with a diminutive fetish-like object. A spell then animates the creation. They do not speak, being silent and creepy horrors that haunt the forest.

The wicker ones are dormant during daylight hours, only acting if disturbed. See **Wicker Ones** (page 162) for the profile.

### Combat with a Wicker One

The wicker ones fall dormant during daylight hours, but if disturbed, they attack—however, all their combat rolls are made with a penalty die during daylight hours, and their MOV rate is reduced to 7 (see **Wicker Ones** , page 162).

The wicker ones aim to grab the scout-investigators and carry them off. If outmatched and injured, wicker ones attempt to climb a tree to escape.

- Call for a **Cool** roll when encountering wicker ones.

Keeper note: the optional Luck Spending rule (page 41) gives players the chance to adjust poor skill rolls; should events lead to the worst, the **Cheating Death** rule (page 42) can save their scout-investigator from certain death.

### Studying a Lifeless Wicker One

If scout-investigators kill a wicker one, they see that something hides within the woven branches, and they realize it’s a human corpse! Hundreds of short

branches and vines woven around and through its bones form a macabre scarecrow, with branches growing out of the mouth and eye sockets.

If they unknot or cut through the branches, they find the corpse’s heart removed, and an odd, fetishlike construct stuffed inside the cavity. This construct consists of a knot of twine wrapped around a smaller heart, that of a bird or rodent.

**Keeper note:** the first wicker one encountered contains the corpse of missing Albany scout, Squire Cuthbert—for information about Cuthbert, see the **Library** (page 148).

- Removing the fetish-construct breaks the spell animating the wicker one; it falls apart, becoming merely sticks and bones.

On Brumsted’s “death,” a bright yellow light glows from his heart, while a swirling, ice-blue, magical Gate opens behind him. Within seconds, the Gate expands to 10 feet (3 m) wide, and a violent wind begins to blow into the Gate. The howl is deafening—the howl of the Windwalker! Through the Gate, scout-investigators see the terrifying icy wastes the Windwalker calls home. Brumsted’s remains are sucked through and scattered across the ice, into oblivion.

- Some crowd members get swept up by the wind and blown into the Gate; others leap through willingly, ecstatically praising the Windwalker’s name as they go.
- Scout-investigators know to keep away from the Gate and, ideally, grab hold of something to avoid being swept in. Call for a **STR** roll to resist being pulled through the Gate—if they seize something solid, grant one bonus die.
- After one round, the Gate closes.

**Keeper note:** if any scout-investigators are swept through the Gate, consider delaying the Gate’s closure a few seconds. This allows time for attempts to save their friend; perhaps they hold hands and form a chain or find a rope to help and pull them free just as the Gate closes. If the scouts work together, no roll is needed. However, the Keeper may require the unfortunate scout-investigator to spend their Luck to return, using the **Cheating Death** rule (page 42).

### Defeating Cline

Cline is prepared to do anything to protect Brumsted. He has several spells at his command, see **Thomas Cline’s** profile (page 60). He can use **Enthrall Victim** (page 170) if he faces more than one or two targets or wishes a more subtle effect. As an overt attack, he can employ **Tongue of the Woods** (page 172).

### Cline Blessed by the Windwalker

After descending to the ground on a beam of light (see **Brumsted’s Ascension** , page 156), Cline’s power receives a boost from the Windwalker’s blessing:

- His POW increases to 90 (an additional 6 magic points).

- **Armor:** 1-point; Cline’s skin hardens and becomes bluish.

- His STR increases to 75 and his SIZ to 70 (Build increases to 1, damage bonus to +1D4, and hit points to 12).

- **Piercing Cold Attack:** Cline can now inflict freezing damage. If he grabs a target with a combat maneuver, each round following where he maintains his frigid grip, he inflicts 1D3+1 damage as he saps his target’s body heat. Those losing half their hit points to this attack must make a **CON** roll: failure leads to frostbite and permanent loss of 1D10 CON.

### Running the Final Scene

All the various elements are here—the creepy house, the crowd outside, head-cultist Cline, undead Brumstead, and the ritual. But how the scene unfolds depends on the scout-investigators’ approach to this point and how you choose to handle those pieces. Some possible ways appear below as guidance.

### Arriving Early

The crowd is starting to gather outside the house as the scout-investigators arrive.

- If they wait and watch, Cline and Brumsted come out and the ritual begins—play it out as described in **Arriving in the Nick of Time** (following).
- If scout-investigators break into the house, they may confront Cline in the laboratory while he is **Raising Brumsted** (page 156). This probably results in a fight against Cline and the prematurely reanimated Brumsted.

- One option is to have some of Cline’s Henchmen (page 162) come to Cline’s aid; in which case, they aim to capture, rather than kill, the scout-investigators, who find themselves forced to watch the ceremony. As described in **Brumsted’s Ascension** (page 156), when


> — SHADOW OVER WESTHAVEN

the Windwalker appears, the crowd panics and the scout-investigators regain their freedom—prior to this, consider allowing those with **Knot-Tying Badges** (page 32) to automatically free themselves, and/or a Hard **STR** or **DEX** roll to free their bonds.

### Arriving in the Nick of Time

The scout-investigators arrive just as Cline and Brumsted exit the house to commence their ritual (see **Brumsted’s Ascension** , page 156). Consider this the simpler, default ending; it plays out as described in the preceding text, with the onus on players to stop events progressing too far.

### Evening the Odds

The scout-investigators look to be outmatched, but they do have some things that can help:

- See **Boons** (page 159) for a list of items that they may be able to use.

- The optional Luck Spending rule (page 41) gives players the chance to adjust poor skill rolls, and the **Cheating Death** rule (page 42) can save scoutinvestigators from certain death.

### Gustaf & Sydney Reappear

If the Windwalker scooped these two up from the **Woodland Altar** (page 143) at Muskrat Lake, it could, optionally, deposit them where and when the Keeper wishes—to either thwart or aid the scout-investigators:

- If the scout-investigators are winning too easily, introduce Gustaf and Sydney to aid Cline; or perhaps they supplant him, taking over the ritual to gain the Windwalker’s blessing for themselves.

### Conclusion

If all goes well, the scout-investigators saved Westhaven, and the shadow is dispelled. The town returns to normal. People go about their business and quickly forget what took place. While no one understands why, the townsfolk now treat the scout-investigators differently, more like equals. Passing by Harley’s Sweet Shoppe, Bob is replacing a windowpane and greets them warmly. The council president, Mr. Booker, thanks them most sincerely, and adds that he hopes he can rely on their votes.

Only their old friend Boxcar Jim is nowhere to be seen.

Despite this general memory loss, the scout-investigators remember what happened: do they keep this a secret among themselves?

**Keeper note:** if the scout-investigators failed to stop Cline, he remains in charge, the townsfolk still under his spell. Thus, Westhaven develops a foul reputation. The overt horrors hide in the shadows, and the town returns to a semblance of normality; but, behind closed doors, townsfolk live in fear. Who knows what nefarious plans Cline may have… _Mr. James_

### The Old Hanson Place

### Return of the Sons of Seth

This seed works well after the events of **Box of Evil** (page 88). In that scenario, the scout-investigators learned of the secretive cult, the Sons of Seth. Hopefully, they survived the encounter, and the Sons of Seth did not learn of the scout-investigators’ involvement.

Several avenues exist to involve the scouts. The first sees scouts actively investigating the Sons of Seth following those events. With this option, the scouts continue to research historical documents for additional cult background. They may also talk to townsfolk, although a few wrong questions could arouse the Sons’ suspicions.

Another option is for one of the scouts to recognize one of the men who delivered the box to Grimm. Knowing the Sons of Seth’s symbol, they may notice a small tattoo, ring, or piece of jewelry bearing the same symbol. Once identified, the scouts can track the individual and perhaps identify additional names.

This could lead the scouts to make a connection (perhaps via a local contact) with a disgruntled member. This person tells of an upcoming ritual, which may involve a human sacrifice—the horrified individual wants nothing to do with that sort of activity. The source tells them the ceremony’s time Boxcar Jim talks about the “old, haunted Hanson place” and dares the scout-investigators to spend a night. He offers a spell or artifact from his bag of tricks as a reward if they can spend one evening in the house and stay until dawn—this could be something they missed in an earlier scenario that could be useful in the **Shadows Over Westhaven** scenario (see **Boons** , page 159).

Boxcar Jim relates his own experience in the house. He was looking for a place to crash for a while and believed the allegedly abandoned house ideal. He entered and started a fire in the living room fireplace. While warming himself, gnarled faces appeared in the flickering flames. Later, mysterious sounds emanated from throughout the house, but he assumed raccoons caused those. Finally, as he was laying on a tattered couch, trying to sleep, he awoke to a shadow trying to envelop him. Dread filled him, and he fled.

If scout-investigators accept his challenge, they can research the house and its history at the local library, historical society, and ask around town. They find that the “Hanson place” has been abandoned for about 10 years. The previous owners reported strange occurrences immediately after moving in, including objects moving on their own, odd noises, and feelings of dread. They
believed the house was haunted. Before that, the house hosted a young couple, recently moved to Westhaven, who disappeared without a trace.

Further research uncovers ties to one of Westhaven’s dark tragedies. The house was originally built by Peter and Victoria Smythe. During the Revolutionary War, widowed Victoria Smythe lured British soldiers into her barn, trapped them inside, and burned them alive. So, most folk believe the house is haunted by the murdered soldiers’ ghosts; however, the scoutinvestigators discover the truth is much, much worse.

The actual threat at the house is up to you. The ghost may or may not be real. It can be any monster you choose or create.

One suggestion is a formless spawn that lurks in the attic, and can seep into the walls and enter the closets of the entire house. The attic is accessed by a ceiling panel in a second-floor bedroom.

The spawn’s infestation occurred when the young couple owned the house. Ernest Hanson was an Astronomy professor at Middlewich College in Westhaven. He and his wife, Carla, were driving around the countryside stargazing when they witnessed a meteorite land in a field. The couple drove to the landing site. At first, the glowing-hot object was too hot to approach. After a surprisingly brief cool down period, they recovered a grapefruit-sized rock.

When the couple returned home, Ernest spent hours examining the space rock. His inspections with a hammer and chisel resulted in cracking the rock. Annoyed that her husband was spending so much time with the “silly space pebble,” Carla demanded he put it away. He relented and tossed the meteorite in a box in the attic. From there, the formless spawn seeped out of the rock and grew, feeding on bats, bugs, and mice.

Over time, it learned to speak “human” from listening to Carla and Ernest. It began to whisper to the couple, terrifying them and making them doubt their sanity. The spawn enjoyed tormenting the couple; but its real motivation was to make them helpless, so it could eat them. Which it did, leaving no trace.

The scout-investigators can explore the house easily enough. The first floor features a bathroom, dining area, kitchen, living room, sitting room, and closets throughout. The second floor has four bedrooms with closets and a bathroom. All rooms are furnished, but in an abandoned state—moth-eaten, moldy, dusty, and so on. In fact, many mundane items remain, as the previous occupants appear to have left in a hurry.

The scouts can search the house, hang out, play cards, and wait for something to happen. The strangeness starts small with odd creaking noises. Disturbing events escalate as the formless spawn awakens to the lifeforces in the house, causing all manner of weirdness to ensue. The spawn is still not yet fully grown; however, it’s able to extend its body to two rooms at a time, creating distractions intending to lure “food” to where it can attack and consume.

The scouts may be able to scare the spawn away using the Totem (page 171) or harm it using a **Blessed Blade** (page 170), both from **Tremors Below** (page 70). They could also use the **Dust of Suleiman** (page 170) spell from **Box of Evil** (page 88). Or they might simply flee.

**Keeper note:** the classic introductory scenario, _The Haunting_ , would be easy to set in Westhaven with a similar introduction from Boxcar Jim. It’s part of the _**Call of Cthulhu: Quick-Start Rules**_ , a free download at Chaosium.com.

### Strange Days at the Giddy-yap Ranch

This scenario seed focuses on mysterious events at a remote youth-work ranch. The most likely setup has scout-investigators sent to the ranch after the traumatic events of one of the scenarios in this book. Perhaps either authorities don’t believe or blame the scout-investigators for what occurred. Therefore, they get sent to a juvenile rehabilitation center where the situation is far from normal.

The ranch’s location is in western Massachusetts. It appropriates Old West culture and imagery as part of its theme. Alternatively, a desert plain location in the western US would provide maximum imagery. Ultimately, set the ranch somewhere that inspires you.

A standard stint at the ranch lasts four to six weeks, allowing scout-investigators to experience the strangeness and perhaps even thwart it. While the scouts stay there, their day includes three hours of classroom time, one hour of mindfulness training, and five to six hours of work detail. The work details primarily undertake projects around the ranch and include activities like fence repair, building or rebuilding structures, and digging. Lots of digging. Three meals a day are provided, but the food quality leaves a lot to be desired.

The work details are brutal, leaving the scouts exhausted afterward. When they are doing excavation, the digging seems to be focused primarily on certain rocks that have streaks of some unknown minerals in them. When these rocks are found, they immediately get collected by the ranch’s counselors. None provide
any answers as to why the youth are digging, other than it “builds character.”

The scouts interact with various ranch staff, some of whom are friendlier than others. They also get to know some of the other young people who have been sent to the ranch for rehabilitation. From these other youth, the scouts begin hearing tales of mistreatment and other strangeness. They then witness some odd events, themselves. Some youth non-player characters (NPCs) get sent away to solitary detention for disruptive behavior; but when they come back, they act differently, as if not totally themselves, and odd burn marks and puncture holes mar their skin. They seem “out of it,” with no memory of what happened.

The truth is that a mi-go pod has set up a base nearby to conduct experiments on humans. The mi-go co-opted the ranch’s second-in-command, Tex McGreevy, and are conducting experiments on the youth. Scout-investigators must figure out what’s going on and either defeat the mi-go or escape before they become the next victims. Maybe they stumble across a mi-go ray gun to help them…
