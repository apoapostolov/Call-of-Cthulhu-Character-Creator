### Chapter 6: Treasure of the Secret Way

This scenario is playable by up to six scoutinvestigators and takes approximately T one or two sessions to complete. Here, scout-investigators receive a crudely drawn map to a lost gold mine, allowing them to do some treasure hunting. Yet, they aren’t the only ones to have stumbled onto the mine’s location. Conman Silas Ardmore has the same map; he previously approached retired adventurer **Josef Grimm** (see **Box of Evil** , page 88) about helping to bankroll an expedition in return for a cut.

### Keeper Information

In 1846, just before the California gold rush, a German coal miner named Heinrich Strauss emigrated to the US dreaming of a new life. Strauss settled in Westhaven and began to survey the region for mining opportunities. After months of studying geological maps and hiking through the foothills and ranges, Strauss came across a range of rocky hills northwest of town that seemed perfect. He dug a series of test pits and channels, and it didn’t take long to recover a few ounces (100 g) of gold dust. He was going to be rich!

Returning to Westhaven, Strauss formed the Falstaff Mining Company (he was a Shakespeare fan) and began recruiting a team. His mining crew consisted of himself and two other men. Every few weeks, they visited Westhaven for supplies. Strauss took care that no one followed them for fear of claim jumpers. Despite some initial success, as summer turned to fall, tempers began to fray.

Money was running out and everyone was growing frustrated. The small flakes of gold were failing to turn into a bigger find.

Westhaven residents noted Strauss and his crew had stopped coming into town altogether. No one ever saw them again. Rumors circulated that Strauss secretly struck it big and retired in luxury, while other stories had more sinister endings. Eventually, the legend of the “lost prospector” became a myth that has now been retold thousands of times. It eventually became known as the “Treasure of the Secret Way.”

The real story is simple: Strauss fell out with his workers, and a fight broke out. Strauss died alone in the mine, jealously guarding his find. Now, his ghost roams the mine.

Presently, conman Silas Ardmore is secretly working the mine; the last thing he wants is meddling scouts getting in his way. So, not only must the scouts contend with Ardmore, but also with a restless spirit!

### Involving the Scout-investigators

Boxcar Jim presents the scout-investigators with a map that, with a little work on their part, leads them to Strauss’ gold mine. Those who played **Box of Evil** (page 88) may have learned of the **Legend of the Treasure of the Secret Way** (see **Handout: Treasure 2** ); however, lacking the map, this provided only a teaser for this scenario. Now, they’ve remembered that old legend, and being two years older, they feel ready to go exploring for gold.

Ardmore recently finished a two-year sentence for burglary. Armed with a map to Strauss’ old mine, he located and began working the mine, but, so far, with little success. ### Baba Itzak’s Brooch

This old pewter brooch bearing a curious design protects the wearer from psychic attacks—such as those made by Strauss’ ghost. The brooch grants the wearer one bonus die on opposed POW rolls made versus psychic attack, as well as spell attacks.

The brooch is currently in Silas Ardmore‘s possession, but, at a point of the Keeper’s choosing, he loses it—and fails to notice its absence until targeted by the ghost.

Ardmore‘s camp is located over a hill, away from the mine’s main entrance.

- **Description:** hunched, yet average build, with greasy black hair and a short beard; always furtive.

### Leads: Obscure & Obvious Clues

At the end of key sections, we list “leads” to help Keepers run scenarios smoothly. This provides clear routes for scout-investigators to follow in terms of clues and plot progression. The leads tell Keepers which scene or event comes next, navigating the path the story can take from here.

- If the lead is labeled “obvious”—make scoutinvestigators aware of it as the scene progresses. They usually do not require a skill roll. Obvious leads are essential to the plot.
- If the lead is labeled “obscure”—the clue usually requires some form of specific action or a skill roll to access the information; or it may be available from a more easily accessible alternative source. Usually, obscure clues are not necessary to move the scenario forward.

### Wolf Attack

Wolf attacks are rare, as the animals are usually wary of adult humans. But the scouts aren’t adults yet, so the wolves attack any target failing to reach the safety of the mine’s entrance.

Rather than use full combat rules here, when a scout-investigator is attacked, ask if they are dodging or fighting back.

- **Success:** they either avoid the wolf or manage to hit it—either way, they now reach **The Mine Entrance** (following).
- **Failure:** the wolf bites into the scout’s leg, tearing their clothing and causing 1D6 damage. The scout’s screams and flailing limbs scare the wolf into backing away; this allows the scout-investigator to reach **The Mine Entrance** (following).
- Optionally, call for a **Cool** roll for scouts who have a close encounter with a wolf.

So, Strauss spent his final days starving here, alone in the darkness. Brumsted abandoned the mine and spread lies about Strauss’ demise.
- The second item is a tattered leather journal that belonged to Strauss. The contents are in German and difficult to decipher, especially near the end when he was writing in near darkness. Scoutinvestigators with Language (German) can spend a few hours studying to get **Handout: Treasure 3** . Otherwise, wait until later, with more time and a German-to-English dictionary.

- Someone with the Reading Badge (page 33) or the **Signals and Codes Badge** (page 33) can read a few key phrases—enough to know the journal’s writer was betrayed by a man named Brumsted.

**Keeper note:** the gold in this area is fool’s gold (iron pyrite) and worthless.

- **Obvious:** Silas Ardmore arrives to harass the scoutinvestigators—see **Confrontation** , nearby.
- **Obvious:** spending five or more minutes in Area I summons **Heinrich Strauss’ Ghost** (page 128).

### Meeting Ardmore

Keepers can choose how to handle potential meetings with Ardmore, who could be encountered in the mine or at his campsite.

- If Ardmore knows the scouts are approaching, he attempts to hide—ask the scouts to make a **Spot Hidden** roll: with a Hard success, they spot him; otherwise, Ardmore remains hidden and attempts to spook them—choose a **Spooky Event** (page 122).
- If the scouts have just found the “gold” seam in **Area I** , Ardmore takes this opportunity to scare them off—see **Confrontation** (following).
- Ardmore attempts to menace or threaten the scouts with violence.
- Ardmore brokers a deal with the scouts to work together and split the gold. It occurs to Ardmore that the scouts are smaller than him and could squeeze through some of the small spaces in the mine. He plans on double-crossing them, of course.

Ultimately, none of these really change the outcome as the scouts soon face **Heinrich Strauss’ Ghost** (page 128).

### Confrontation

The scouts have little time to evaluate their find in Area I, as Ardmore chooses this moment to try to scare off the interlopers—now, they have “helped him” find the gold. On hearing the scouts talking about gold, he squeezes through the rubble and enters the chamber—in doing this, he unwittingly causes **Baba Itzak’s Brooch** (page 114) to slip off and fall to the ground.

On entering, he sees the gold seam, and a wicked look crosses his face. He doesn’t want to kill the scouts, but he is not averse to hitting them with a stick and yelling heinous threats. A selection of options for what happens are below.

- During the scene, ask for a **Spot Hidden** roll: success notes **Baba Itzak’s Brooch** (page 114) lying on the ground by the entrance.

### _**scouts Flee**_

If the scouts flee, they soon hear screams, as Ardmore is attacked by **Heinrich Strauss’ Ghost** (page 131). As he no longer wears **Baba Itzak’s Brooch** , he is defenseless against the angry spirit. Should the scouts return later, they find Ardmore’s corpse, horror frozen on his gaunt face—the ghost used up its magic points, so it cannot return for several hours.

### _**fighting Ardmore**_

The scouts may attempt an Assisted Fighting Maneuver (page 38), perhaps to restrain Ardmore or push him into **Area H: The Pit** (page 125). If Heinrich is getting the better of the scouts, have **Heinrich Strauss’ Ghost** **127**

### Running the Scene

The two most likely outcomes when encountering the ghost are: **Flee the Ghost** (nearby) or **Fighting the Ghost** (following) or both! Fighting a ghost is tricky, as the scouts soon find out. Strauss’ strong tie to the vein of fool’s gold means he does not pursue anyone outside the mine, and always returns to **Area I** (page 127).

- If scouts have time to gather their wits, ask for an **INT** roll (see **Leap of Logic** , page 47): with success, they realize Strauss’ ghost remains near the gold.
- Keepers can, optionally, have the ghost keep to **Area I** (page 127). While this makes the ghost less of a threat, it also shortens the playing time for those nearing the close of their session.

### Heinrich Strauss’ Ghost

Anyone spending five or more minutes in **Area I** (page 127) sees a strange purple mist begin forming in the chamber’s center. The mist takes on a menacing shape, forming a humanoid with spectral claws. Then, a ghastly skeletal face bursts forth and shrieks at everyone. The mist quickly reforms in the shape of an old miner, wielding a pickaxe.

- Seeing the ghost triggers a **Cool** roll.

### Fighting the Ghost

The ghost makes attacks using an opposed **POW** roll and manifests as swipes with a spectral pickaxe:

- If Strauss’ ghost wins the opposed POW roll: the target feels the pickaxe stab coldly into them—even through it’s not physical—losing 2D10 points of POW. Should a scout reach zero POW, they collapse unconscious and cannot be woken until they have been removed from the mine. POW lost to the ghost is recovered in the **Rewards** section (page 130).
- If Strauss’ ghost loses the opposed POW roll: the pickaxe misses—or passes clean through—and the ghost loses 1D8 magic points. When the ghost reaches zero magic points, it vanishes and cannot return for at least six hours.
- A scout-investigator wearing **Baba Itzak’s Brooch** (page 114) gains one bonus die on a **POW** roll versus the ghost’s attacks.
- If scout-investigators have **Banish the Visitor** (see **Boxcar Jim’s Useful Items** , page 63), they can cast it as a group to trap Strauss’ ghost in the corked glass bottle (as given to them by Boxcar Jim). Award one bonus die if casting in **Area I** (page 127).

Keeper note: the Cheating Death rule (page 42) can save scout-investigators from certain death. Also, note that the glass bottle containing the ghost can prove useful in the final scenes of the following scenario, **Shadow Over Westhaven** (page 132).

### Flee the Ghost

The scout-investigators run. Assuming they still have a light source, they need only win an opposed **DEX** roll versus the ghost’s DEX 45. Any who fail may be
attacked by the ghost. After each such attack, a scout may try to flee again, calling for another opposed **DEX** roll. If working together to help a friend out, Keepers may optionally grant one bonus die to the scout’s DEX roll.

Once each scout has succeeded, they have escaped the ghost and left the mine.

### Conclusion: We Are Rich!

Several possible outcomes follow, depending upon the scout-investigators’ actions.

- **Partial success (with one of DEX or INT):** they seal the mine, but all scouts must make a **Luck** roll to avoid falling rocks (1D4 damage).
- **Failure:** either the dynamite doesn’t go off or only partially blocks the passage.
- **Fumble:** the dynamite explodes while the scouts are in range; everyone makes a **Luck** roll: success inflicts 1D4+1 damage; failure 1D10 damage.

### _**run Away**_

They leave the old mine and go home.

### Strauss Is No More?

If the scouts dealt with Strauss’ ghost, they may now revisit **Area I** (page 127).

### _**blow It Up!**_

The scout-investigators could use the dynamite from **Area F** (page 124) to seal the mine.

- Ask the scout-investigator setting the sweating sticks for a combined **DEX** and **INT** roll (one dice roll compared to both characteristics).
- **Success (with both DEX and INT):** they safely seal the mine.
- Those inspecting the vein of “gold” should make an **Appraise** , Hard **Natural World** , or **Geology** roll. Consider rolling on the player’s behalf and keeping the result concealed, only telling the player the outcome—that makes the investigator’s assumptions more uncertain.
- **Success:** disappointingly, the gold is a pocket of iron pyrite, “fool’s gold,” and of no value.
- **Failure:** gold is gold, right!? Later, when they try to use or spend the gold, they learn the terrible truth.


### Chapter 6

Discovering the “gold” isn’t gold makes the fate of Heinrich Strauss and his mining crew even more tragic.

If they tell the authorities what happened—or at least a version of it—a search party returns to reclaim any bodies.

If scouts tied up Ardmore:

- If the knot-tier has the **Knot-Tying Badge** (page 32), he is securely tied and cannot escape.
- If they don’t have the badge, then ask for a **Luck** roll: failure has Ardmore escape, perhaps troubling the scouts again in the future. Otherwise, he is brought back to town and questioned. He has a poor reputation, and his efforts to incriminate the scouts fail.

- Choose any new **Ability Badge** (page 30).
- POW lost to the ghost is recovered, back to their normal value, over the next week or so.

Grant a **Scout-Investigator Development Phase** (page 47).
