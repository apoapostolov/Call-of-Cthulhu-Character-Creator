### Chapter 2: Rules for Playing Scout-investigators

This chapter explores the game mechanics and options for playing with scoutT investigators. It covers new skills, a modified approach to combat, wounds and healing, character development, and introduces some altered and new rules.

### Combat

_Call of Cthulhu_ investigators face deadly perils, and death and injury can occur; however, for several reasons, combat is handled differently with scout-investigators:

- First, the tone of the game is intentionally different: think young adult fiction, and less brutal.
- Second, many scout-investigators have a negative Damage Bonus (DB). Attacks made by an unarmed person deal 1D3 damage: 2 points of damage being average. A person with Build –2 has their damage reduced by 2 points. Scout-investigators often begin with a DB of –2. This means they deliver zero damage on average—some players may feel frustrated by this.
- Third, these combat rules for scout-investigators are designed to promote teamwork: encouraging the players to work together in creative, enjoyable ways.

### Fighting Maneuvers

_Campfire Tales_ allows scout-investigators to lend a helping hand during maneuvers—something the regular rules do not allow.

The leader’s Build cannot be increased by more than 3 points. So, if the lead scout has Build – 2, their maximum Build is + 1 (negative 2 plus 3 equals 1). This means for assisted fighting maneuvers, four scouts is optimal.

**Note:** this method of increasing Build value is purposefully limited—otherwise, we might see a pile of 11-year-olds wrestling Great Cthulhu to the ground!

Next, compare the lead scout-investigator’s modified Build with their opponent’s to determine the number of penalty dice (see **Fighting Maneuvers** , nearby):

- If the lead scout-investigator’s modified Build is 3 or more points lower than their opponent’s, the maneuver fails.
- If the lead scout-investigator’s modified Build is 2 points lower than their opponent’s, apply two penalty dice.
- If the lead scout-investigator’s modified Build is 1 point lower than their opponent’s, apply one penalty die.
- If the lead scout-investigator’s modified Build equals their opponent, no modifiers are applied.
- If the lead scout-investigator has a modified Build greater than their opponent’s, the maneuver roll is made with one bonus die (no more than one bonus die is awarded by this method).

A scout-investigator cannot assist in a fighting maneuver if they have already taken their action for the present combat round. However, they can delay their action to assist an ally’s fighting maneuver on that ally’s turn in the combat round. So, everyone who assists in a combat maneuver must have held their previous action.

> _**Example** : three scout-investigators, Suzy, Ted, and Ayan have been caught taking an artifact from Colonel Grimm’s study. Mrs. Habersham is approaching as if to grab one of the scout-investigators. The scouts want to push Mrs. Habersham into the broom closet and wedge the door shut. The Keeper begins a combat round and establishes the DEX order: Suzy first (Fighting skill is used as the tiebreaker between her and Ayan, who have equal DEX), then Ayan, then Mrs. Habersham, and finally, Ted._

### _in Dex Order:_

- _Suzy: DEX 70, Fighting (Brawl) 40%, Build – 1._

- _Mrs. Habersham, the housekeeper: DEX 50, Fighting 25%, Build 0._

- _Ted: DEX 40, Fighting (Brawl) 25%, Build – 2._

> _Suzy could attempt the fighting maneuver alone; but her Build is one below that of Mrs. Habersham, so her Fighting roll would be made with a penalty die. She decides to hold her action so that she can assist either Ted or Ayan._

> _Ayan goes next. Now, the scouts face a dilemma. They would like to have Ted team up with them, but Mrs. Habersham acts before Ted. They decide to act on Ayan’s turn._

> _Suzy held her action, so she could lead the maneuver. Her Build increases by 1 (Ayan is helping) to 0, the same as Mrs. Habersham; so, she would roll her Fighting skill of 40% with zero penalty dice. If Ayan leads the maneuver, his Build increases by 1 (Suzy is helping) to + 1, 1 above Mrs. Habersham’s; so, he would roll his Fighting skill of 25% with one bonus die._

> _Which do they prefer? 40% with zero bonus die or 25% with one bonus die? It’s up to them, and they decide to go with Ayan leading the fighting maneuver._

### Cumbersome Weapons

Many weapons are heavy, cumbersome objects. In an adult’s hands, this doesn’t pose a problem, but such items can challenge scout-investigators. Most weapons, except a small knife or light club, count as cumbersome for this purpose. Individual cases can be decided at the Keeper’s discretion.

### Encouraging Assisted Fighting Maneuvers

By teaming up on fighting maneuvers, a group of scout-investigators should prove effective in combat. Encourage creativity in how they use fighting maneuvers, and remember to set clear goals when making them.

- **Failure:** apply a penalty die to their Fighting attack roll.

Examples goals for fighting maneuvers:

- Throw a bedsheet over the enemy to entangle them, giving the scouts time to make a getaway.
- Push their enemy down the stairs, down a well, or through a doorway—potentially inflicting harm.
- Run around their enemy with ropes, wrapping them until they are restrained.
- Jump on their enemy and pin them down.
- Attack with sticks as a group, not to inflict damage, but to frighten their enemy into running away.

All goals are negotiated between the players and the Keeper. For example, the final goal above, scaring an enemy away with sticks, might work on Rusty Smith (see **Smith Bros. ### Faster Recovery

Wounded scout-investigators recover quicker: natural healing is increased to 2 hit points per day of rest. In addition, hit points granted for a successful First Aid or Medicine roll is increased to 1D4.

### Ignore Major Wounds

Do not use the major wounds rule for scout-investigators.

### Zero Hit Points

### Luck Spending

We recommend the optional Spending Luck rule (see _Call of Cthulhu: Keeper Rulebook_ , page 99) when running _Campfire Tales_ . Besides calling for Luck rolls to determine the fickle hand of fate, players can also spend Luck to adjust dice rolls.

Often, this changes failure into success, but Luck can also be spent to alter the degree of success (from Regular to Hard, or from Hard to Extreme). To improve a skill roll, the player “burns” Luck points, spending the amount necessary to reverse a failure (or increase the level of success). Luck points cannot be spent on Luck or Cool rolls.

For example, if you have Climb 50% but rolled 55 (a failure), you could spend 5 Luck to adjust the roll to 50 (a success). The only limit on Luck spending is the “current Luck” held by the scout-investigator; be aware that Luck rolls made later in the game use the new lower value!

Scout-investigators regain spent Luck points at the start of a new scenario by rolling against their current Luck value: if they fail the roll, they gain +1D10 points. Otherwise, if the roll is equal to or less than the scout-investigator’s current Luck score, no points are recovered.

At zero hit points, a scout-investigator automatically falls unconscious. Record the Current Hit Points as “0,” rather than using negative values. The only exception is when a scout-investigator suffers a single wound that deals equal to or greater than their maximum hit point total (see **Dying** , nearby).

In certain circumstances, such as a monster consuming or completely ripping the scout-investigator apart, the Keeper may rule death seems inevitable— the scout-investigator’s only option is **Cheating Death** (page 42).

### Dying

A scout-investigator who suffers a single wound equal to or greater than their maximum hit point total is “Dying” and automatically falls unconscious.  Record the Current Hit Points as “D” for dying rather than a number. To return to play, a “Dying” character must receive treatment—a successful First Aid or Medicine roll:

- **Successful First Aid roll:** the patient gains 1D4 hit points (an Extreme success automatically grants 4 HP recovery). Remove the “D” for “dying,” and enter the number of hit points recovered as their current hit point score.

### Wounds & Healing

Scout-investigators generally have fewer hit points than their adult counterparts. To make them more durable, _Campfire Tales_ adopts some _Pulp Cthulhu_ rules; this allows scout-investigators to shake off wounds **41**

- **Following a successful First Aid roll:** a follow-up Medicine roll may be made later, granting an additional 1D4 hit points (an Extreme success granting 4 hit points recovery).
- First Aid and Medicine rolls cannot increase a patient’s hit points beyond their maximum hit point total.

Once brought back into play, the scout-investigator’s natural healing continues at the rate of 2 hit points per day of rest until they return to their maximum hit point total.

### Wounds & Healing Checklist

**Healing:** 2 HP per day. **First Aid/Medicine:** 1D4 HP. **Major Wounds:** ignore. **Zero HP:** unconscious. **Dying:** single wound equal/greater than max HP. **Cheating Death:** spend all Luck (min. 30 points).

A “Dying” scout-investigator has the option of **Cheating Death** (nearby)—provided they possess sufficient Luck points.

### Cheating Death

If a scout-investigator possesses at least 30 Luck points, they can cheat death. Scout-investigators must spend _all_ their Luck (30 points minimum) to avoid death.

In such situations, the scout-investigator’s Luck value is reduced to zero; their condition slightly improves, and they get to live. The character immediately regains 1D6+1 hit points (not exceeding their maximum hit point total). The player must explain how their scout-investigator escapes the worst of their injuries and survives.

### Death

If a “Dying” scout-investigator does not receive successful medical attention within the hour, and lacks the Luck points for **Cheating Death** (nearby), the character dies.

### Cool

Sanity plays a central role in _Call of Cthulhu,_ but _Campfire Tales_ replaces it with Cool. When an adult investigator would make a Sanity roll, a scoutinvestigator makes a Cool roll instead. Do they “keep cool?” Or do they “lose their cool?”

A scout-investigator’s Cool value is equal to their POW (it does not rise and fall as Sanity does). When making a Cool roll, a roll equal to or below their Cool value succeeds. A success indicates that the scout-investigator took the event in stride and avoided distress. Failing a Cool roll always evokes a response. The nature of this response can vary greatly and depends on the situation. How scout-investigators respond to failing a Cool roll is up to the player.

The “five Fs” below provide excellent starting points. However, allow players to come up with something else if they wish.

### Distress Boxes

- **Bump into something or someone:** crashes into an oil lamp, vase, fellow scout-investigator, etc.
- **Drop something:** drops something they were holding (flashlight, glasses, key, etc.).
- **Make noise:** makes a hullabaloo, perhaps saying something inappropriate, a scream, etc.

**Note:** the involuntary action should be only last a moment. The Keeper does not control the scoutinvestigator for any longer than that. Also, note that that involuntary actions are not mandatory every time someone fails a Cool roll.

### Adversity

Wayfarer Scouts embrace the challenges of hiking and camping in the great outdoors. But just what are these challenges, and how can the Keeper bring them into play?

Adversity in _Campfire Tales_ comes in five forms: cold, hunger, lost, overburdened, and sore. Each form of adversity has an associated dice roll or effect. Ask scouts who succeed in their dice roll about how they overcome the adversity; should they fail, discuss all adverse effects they experience. Either way, the adversity should feed into the story.

_Tired Feet_ **44**

As Keeper, use these rules to remind the players of the challenges they face, but avoid overuse—otherwise, players may feel they are being punished.

Scout-investigator sheets feature a checkbox for each of the five forms of adversity. Players should check a box when their scout-investigator fails the roll associated with that form of adversity; erase the checkmark when they are restored (see the various forms of **Restoration** , following).

- If a scout-investigator has 3 adversities checked, Cool rolls are made with one penalty die.
- If a scout-investigator has all 5 adversities checked, Cool rolls are made with two penalty dice.

### Cold

Keeping warm can be a constant battle in the winter, especially outdoors. Appropriate clothing and hot food can do a lot to warm a person. Moving around helps too—a person soon gets cold if stationary, especially if sitting or lying on the ground. Without food, the body lacks fuel to generate body heat. Exposure to cold water or wet clothes saps body heat rapidly.

When cold is constant, the Keeper should call for just enough “Adversity: Feeling Cold” rolls to remind players of their environment.

### _**adversity: Feeling Cold**_

When scout squads spend prolonged periods in the cold without proper gear, the Keeper can ask for a **Survival** roll. Use the scout with the highest skill. Optionally, apply one penalty die in freezing weather.

- **Success:** the scouts find a way to keep warm; ask the players how they have managed to keep warm: what shelter have they made; what have they found to eat; have they managed to make a fire?
- **Failure:** the scouts’ teeth begin to chatter, and the cold gnaws at their bones. No one recovers any hit points (see **Wounds & Healing** , page 41) until they warm up. Also, if the weather is freezing, each scoutinvestigator loses 1 hit point, and makes a CON roll—if failed, they develop a nasty cold.

### _**restoration**_

Donning warm, dry clothing; eating hot food; sitting by a fire for 30 minutes.

### Hunger

The body burns many calories when hiking, and a hearty meal does a lot to raise morale. Even one missed meal can cause frayed tempers. Food also helps the body generate heat, and in low temperatures, people need to eat to stay warm.

### _**adversity: Feeling Hungry**_

After a scout squad misses a couple of meals, the Keeper can ask for a **Survival** roll. Use the scout with the highest skill. Apply one penalty die if the land is barren.

- **Success:** the scouts find food; ask the players what they have found (add some description—did it need preparation?)
- **Failure:** the scouts’ bellies rumble. After a day of this, ask for a **Cool** roll—if failed, tempers fray. Also, no one recovers any hit points (see **Wounds & Healing** , page 41) until they eat.

### _**restoration**_

Eat something. A hearty cooked meal is best, but anything tastes delicious when you are hungry.

### Lost

Wayfarer Scouts learn orienteering: navigating using maps and a compass. Long hikes are hard work; going the wrong way or finding you’ve gone in a circle and ended up where you began demoralizes you. Picture yourself, lost in the wilderness; it’s starting to rain, and the sun quickly sets behind the trees—getting lost can be soul-destroying.

### _**adversity: Feeling Lost**_

When a scout becomes lost in the wilderness, after failing a Navigate roll, the Keeper can ask for a **Cool** roll. Apply one penalty die if a scout is alone.

- **Success:** the scout keeps their morale up; ask them how—perhaps they sing a cheery walking song?
- **Failure:** the scout loses their cool; the player must describe how—perhaps they become upset, break something, or head off alone. Do they split up or make enough noise to be heard by something or someone?

### _**restoration**_

Finding a familiar location, perhaps a road or the edge of town, and knowing that they are no longer lost can put a smile back on the scouts’ faces. Of course, a person can be lost in a city, but the same applies. Running across a friendly person who knows where they are can also dispel the sense of being lost.

### Overburdened

When hiking, Wayfarer Scouts carry what they need in a backpack: a sleeping bag, camping equipment, food, dry clothes, etc. The pack may not feel too heavy when starting, but it feels much heavier after hiking a few miles. Supporting or carrying a friend can be an additional burden. Running away while overburdened is near impossible.

- If the scout-investigator carries a full pack (including tent and sleeping bag): reduce MOV by 3.
- The Keeper should adjudicate other burdens, using the above as a guide.

### _**restoration**_

If a scout-investigator needs to run, their only option may be to dump their gear. Of course, this means that later, they don’t have needed items such as dry clothes or food. Jettisoning a pack only takes a moment, and the action does not slow the scout-investigator down.

### Adversity Checklist

**Cold:** Survival roll. **Hunger:** Survival roll. **Lost:** Cool roll. **Overburdened:** modify MOV rate (–1 or –3). **Sore:** CON roll. **Three checked:** one penalty die on Cool rolls. **All five checked:** two penalty dice on Cool rolls.

### _**adversity: Feeling Sore**_

Sometimes scouts push themselves too hard, such as long hike with insufficient rest stops, or hours at a hard physical task unfamiliar to them. When a scoutinvestigator overexerts themselves, ask for a **CON** roll:

- **Success:** the scout is tired, but otherwise, fine.
- **Failure:** the scout feels sore somehow; discuss the specifics with the player—does the scoutinvestigator have blisters, for example. This outcome feeds into the roleplaying, rather than any game penalty—significant injury is gauged in hit points, but items like splinters and blisters don’t merit a damage roll. If the Keeper feels it’s appropriate to the story, apply one penalty die to any subsequent Cool roll for that scout-investigator.

“Feeling Sore” rolls could be appropriate in other situations, such as:

- Forcing one’s way through thick undergrowth may require a STR roll. Afterward, a CON roll might determine if they are sore from scratches and cuts.
- If a DEX roll is failed while wading a stream, one might slip and fall. Afterward, a CON roll might determine to see if they are sore from bruising.
- Next to lakes or swamps in warm conditions, Keepers might call for a Luck roll to see if scouts get bitten by insects. Afterward, a CON roll might determine if they are sore with itchy bites.

- If the scout has slept outside at night without their camping gear, a CON roll might determine if they are sore from lying on tree roots or hard ground.

### Leap of Logic

The scout-investigators begin as naïve 11-year-olds. But in TV and films, the kids sometimes connect the dots in ways that leave viewers wondering, “ _How on Earth did they figure that out?”_ The game refers to this as a “Leap of Logic.” When the Keeper calls for a Leap of Logic roll, all scout-investigators should make an INT roll.

- A Leap of Logic allows Keepers to tell players facts they might not otherwise know or figure out (such as a clue).
- The scout-investigator with best level of success makes the leap of logic. In case of a draw, the highest INT value wins.
- If everyone fails the INT roll, the Keeper decides the outcome. They may still wish to give the investigator with the highest INT a hint—that scout-investigator should now be feeling, _“Wait, I had it for a moment—let me think…,”_ with the Keeper feeding hints as they see fit.

### Scout-investigator Development Phase

A scout-investigator begins at the age of 11 or 12 as a Wanderer. After one or more sessions, the Keeper can announce that time has passed, and the scout progresses to Rover; they are now 13–14 years old. If this were a TV show, it would be a following season; both actor and character would have aged a year or two.

The scouts grow, both physically and mentally, and three characteristics (Strength, Size, and Education) may alter at each step. The scouts are also learning rapidly; this is reflected in additional skill points available to them.

### Education & Own Language Skill

The **“** Own Language” skill begins equal to the scout’s EDU characteristic, which can increase during the scout-investigator development phase. Be sure to add all increases in the EDU characteristic to the scout’s Own Language skill.

> _**Example: Bobby’s Own Language (English) starts equal to EDU 22. During character creation, he spends 8 skill points increasing Own Language to 30. When progressing from__**Wanderer to Rover** (nearby), his EDU increases by 5 points to 27. So, he raises his Own Language (English) skill by 5 points to 35._

### Progressing: Wanderer to Rover

Roll for the following changes when moving from Wanderer to Rover (becoming 13–14 years old):

- Strength (STR): add 5 to the scout’s present STR value. Then, roll 2D6+ 3, and multiply the result by 3. If the product exceeds the scout’s STR, use the new value.

- Size (SIZ): roll 2D6+ 5 and multiply the result by 3. If the product exceeds the scout’s SIZ, use the new value.

- Education (EDU): add 5 to the scout’s present EDU value. Then, roll 2D6+ 3, and multiply the result by 3. If the product exceeds the scout’s EDU, use the new value.

**Note:** applying the changes above guarantees a 5-point minimum increase in Strength and Education. However, no similar Size increase guarantee exists. Be sure to check for any changes in hit points, Build, damage bonus, and Own Language skill after increasing characteristics.

- The scout-investigator may choose to continue with the same hobby or start a new one. A scout can have only one hobby at a time.

### Progressing: Rover to Ranger

Roll for the following changes when moving from Rover to Ranger (becoming 15–16 years old):

### A Note About Using Adults in These Stories

While most gamers are accustomed to playing adult characters, this book focuses on youth characters as protagonists. The adults are, by design, pushed to the periphery of the story. It may be tempting to introduce a playable scout leader or other adult into the mix. However, this focuses on one investigator as the _de facto_ leader. This can lead to an uneven style of play where the other players feel marginalized. The intent of this material is for scout-investigators to all be peers and work as a team to achieve their goals.

For that reason, we recommend restricting adults to use as non-player characters (NPCs) in these stories. This may include parents, scout leaders, police, and town residents. The focus of this game is the scouts: those young people, out camping in the wilderness, telling their _Campfire Tales_ .

### Alternative Characteristic Rolls

Note that the dice rolls for adult investigators’ characteristics provided in this chapter differ from the ones provided in regular _Call of Cthulhu._

- The rolls presented here are 2D6+ 5 multiplied by 5, giving characteristics a range of 35 to 85, with an average of 60.

- The rolls in regular _Call of Cthulhu_ are different: five of the characteristics (STR, CON, DEX, APP, and POW) use 3D6 multiplied 5, giving a range of 15 to 90, with an average of 52.5; the other three (SIZ, INT, and EDU) use 2D6+6 multiplied 5, giving a range 40 to 90, with an average of 65. The average over the eight characteristics using that method is just over 57, so the difference between the two methods is minimal.

Those who wish to avoid the lower end (30 and lower) may prefer to use the rolls provided here. This method uses the same rolls for all eight characteristics, which makes the process a little more straightforward.

### Downtime Activity

The scout-investigators are assumed to live in Westhaven, and life goes on between scenarios. Depending on how the story unfolds around your gaming table, some loose ends or unresolved issues may remain after completing a scenario.

Focusing on characters and player-led investigation is strongly encouraged and can help make a game come alive. The Keeper may wish to incentivize participation in downtime activity by rewarding scout-investigators with an additional **Ability Badge** (page 30). Some topics that downtime activity might address include the following:

- Activities centered around the scout-investigator’s **Obligations** (page 28) and the people associated with them.
- Following up on previous scenarios: at the library, bookshops, or with other townspeople.
- Home life: involving other family members.
- Life around town: involving other kids and regular townsfolk.
- School life: involving teachers or other pupils.
- Scouting life: fundraising (litter picking, sponsored events, etc.), camping, hiking, etc.

A popular method with some groups is to focus a short scene on one scout-investigator at a time. This allows their fellow players to roleplay other characters in the scene. Some players enjoy the chance to play someone else’s mother or brother, or fellow school children. This can provide a lot of humor, but can also be played straight. There should be little need for the use of character sheets or dice rolls. Alternatively, rather than roleplaying, a player may prefer to describe a scene involving their scout-investigator and something they did between adventures.

Whichever approach you use, create a rich story and shine a spotlight on the scout-investigators’ lives.
