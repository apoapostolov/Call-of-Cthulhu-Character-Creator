### Chapter 4: Tremors Below

This makes an ideal beginning scenario for scout-investigators starting out as T Wanderers. Playable by up to six scoutinvestigators, it takes approximately one or two sessions to complete.

Here, while on a series of day hikes working toward the **Hiking Badge** (page 32), they encounter a terror from beneath the earth. As they try to keep calm and escape the forest, they must deal with an increasing number of terrifying events. This ultimately leads to the realization they are not alone in the woods.

Eventually, they discover a remote cabin, which may end up being their salvation—or their last stand!

### Keeper Information

This is a story of revenge. Norma Gross threatened to expose the local cult (see **Sons of Seth** , page 60); this led the cult leader, **Thomas Cline** (page 60), to make an example of Gross and send a warning to others about crossing the cult. Entering the woods near her cabin, Cline summoned an underworld monster to consume Gross.

A day later, a burrowing assassin emerged in the deep forests. Through a combination of magic and telepathy, Cline steered the monster toward Gross—this was no easy task, and an innocent farmer suffered an untimely death prior to the monster finding its target.

This scenario’s events take place in October 1918. Far away, the Great War is entering its final month. The mild weather is ideal for hiking. The nights are cooler, but not yet freezing.

### The Monster

Burrowing assassins dwell underground. They typically surface only when summoned or when they have business with a surface dweller. Cline psychically projects images of Gross into the creature’s mind. However, all humans look the same to the poor burrowing assassin, and it picks off a local farmer before it finds Gross at her cabin. So, it develops a taste for soft, squishy creatures.

The creature approaches Gross while she’s working outside, but her loyal dog, Katy, manages to distract the monster and draw it away. Gross is no fool and begins creating a protective totem. But before she completes her arcane ward, fear overwhelms her, and she grabs her shotgun and locks herself in her pantry. Unfortunately, the stress induces a heart attack, and Gross dies in the closet. Katy escapes and roams the woods, barking furiously every time she feels the assassin’s vibrations moving nearby.

### Burrowing Assassin

A hideous gray worm, some 10 feet (3 m) long. It burrows through earth like a hot knife through butter. It lacks eyes but seems to scent its prey with a mass of facial tentacles. A sucker-like mouth lies in the middle of the writhing tentacles, ready to suck the living juices from whatever it can grasp. For the full profile, see **Burrowing Assassin**, page 87.

### Leads: Obscure & Obvious Clues

At the end of key sections, we list “leads” to help Keepers run scenarios smoothly. This provides clear routes for scout-investigators to follow in terms of clues and plot progression. The leads tell Keepers which scene or event comes next, navigating the path the story can take from here.

- If the lead is labeled “obvious”—make scoutinvestigators aware of it as the scene progresses. They usually do not require a skill roll. Obvious leads are essential to the plot.
- If the lead is labeled “obscure”—the clue usually requires a specific action or skill roll to access the information; or it may be available from a more easily accessible alternative source. Usually, obscure clues are not necessary to move the scenario forward.

### Start: Scrap Metal Challenge

The scenario begins in the Westhaven scout hut. The scout-investigators’ squad are all present.

Paul Glenn (page 56), the scout leader, asked them to think about the latest challenge. **The Wayfarer Scouts** (page 29) have been collecting scrap metal for weeks and selling it to **Smith Bros. Salvage** (page 64) as a fundraiser for orphans. The squad that raises the most money wins an ice cream social at **Harley’s Sweet Shoppe** (page 56).

Currently, our scout-investigators are tied with **The Stumbling Moose Squad** (page 65).

**Keeper note:** this challenge showcases the charity work that is an important part of scouting. It also provides opportunities for roleplaying and introducing some townsfolk—a chance for scout-investigators to interact with their adult contacts, especially if that contact might know a source of scrap metal. Refer to the scout-investigators’ obligations (see **Hobbies** , page 17) to paint a fuller picture of their lives.

### Individual Effort

Ask each scout-investigator what they are doing to acquire scrap metal.

This being their first time playing in Westhaven, players won’t be familiar with the locations and people, so give them a map (see **Handout: Westhaven 1** ). Encourage them to discuss the types of places they might go, and then refer to the setting guide in **Chapter 3: Westhaven** as necessary, using players’ suggestions. Feel free to create new people and places. Encourage players to add labels to the map and make it their own—for a start, they can decide their home’s location.

Allow each scout-investigator one skill roll to gauge their effectiveness in collecting scrap metal. Keepers decide which skill seems most appropriate for the player’s chosen action. For example, if the plan centers around talking, ask for a social skill roll ( **Charm** , **Fast Talk** , **Intimidate** , or **Persuade)** . If a scout comes up with an exceptionally strong or entertaining idea, consider awarding one bonus die to the roll.

- **Success:** the plan works—invite the player to describe the outcome; this counts as one success for the scout-investigators’ squad in the scrap challenge.
- **Failure:** the plan fails—ask the player what might have gone wrong; this counts as one success for the Stumbling Mooses in the scrap challenge.
- **Fumble or failed pushed roll:** the scout-investigator either sours their relationship with their **Trusted Adult** (page 25), or they inadvertently help the Stumbling Mooses acquire more metal; counting as one success for the Stumbling Moose squad.

Keep a tally of player successes or failures. When all the players have made a skill roll, total up the successes to determine a winner. No rolls are made for the Stumbling Moose squad—the players’ dice rolls determine the outcome. If it ends in a tie, ask for a group **Luck** roll to determine the winner.

**Keeper note:** this challenge is presented as an opportunity for roleplay and fun—the dice rolls are secondary. This is a chance to get to know the scoutinvestigators, Westhaven, and some of its residents.

### Planning the Hike

The Westhaven Unit # 104 scout leaders have settled on a series of five 20 mile (32 km) loops through OrthBeane Forest Preserve; this large, densely forested area sits some 12 miles (19 km) northeast of Westhaven. Once the five loops have been completed— 100 miles (161 km) over five weekends—the scouts receive their coveted **Hiking Badge** (page 32).

The list of equipment for this series of hikes includes a canteen, a first aid kit, a flashlight, trail snacks, and a knife. Blankets and ponchos are recommended in case they need to sleep out for a night. However, they are expected to finish each 20 mile (32 km) segment in a day.

**Keeper note:** apart from the start in Westhaven, this scenario does not use a map for the Orth-Beane Forest Preserve; instead, present a dense forest through which a main trail already exists, with many smaller and often obscured trails running throughout (easy to get lost). Follow the sequence of scenes rather than a map, but allow players to draw their own map based on their actions and experiences.


### Tremors Below

### Hey, Kids! It’s Boxcar Jim!

As the scouts walk through town, perhaps after buying supplies at **Bailey’s Top-Quality Hardware** (page 58) or after celebrating at **Harley’s Sweet Shoppe** (page 56), a familiar face appears. It is the strange drifter and selfdescribed “friend of the scouts,” **Boxcar Jim** (page 62).

Jim’s eyes dart about quickly, scanning the area as he talks. Read or paraphrase the following aloud:

“ _Oh, you are all okay! Thank goodness! I had a dream last night. At least, I think it was a dream. Are we dreaming now? No! This is real. It was a nightmare, a real doozy. And you were in it. You too_ (points to each scout) _. You were all in it!_

> _“We were all hanging out in Central Park. You all were playing baseball, and I was napping on the grass with a full belly. Suddenly, a huge earthquake made the ground around us swirl like a whirlpool! We got sucked down deep underground into a dark cavernous world. It was hot, and rivers of lava flowed around us. Suddenly, tentacles burst from the rocks and bound us all. Small maggot-like worms then emerged from pores in the tentacles and burrowed into our bodies, slowly devouring us.”_

Jim shudders, wipes the sweat off his brow and pulls his sticky hair to one side.

> _“I think I may have overstayed my welcome here. The store owners have been running me off, and it is going to turn colder soon. Fall comes soon, and then, winter. I need to head south and winter somewhere warm like Florida. Maybe it was because of my nightmare, but I feel like I should give you something from my bag of tricks. Hopefully, it keeps you safe.”_

Jim hands them a folded-up piece of weathered oiled canvas, bound by twine. Written on the canvas is the spell **Bless Blade** (see **Boxcar Jim’s Useful Items** , page 63). But rather than calling it “Bless Blade,” Jim named it “Mighty Sharp.”

Jim asks the scouts where they are going this weekend. When they tell him, he looks deeply concerned. He digs in his pack again and pulls out a newspaper, pointing to an article—give players **Handout: Tremors 1** . He then says:

> _“I don’t know if this has anything to do with my nightmares, but you guys please be careful out there!”_

- **Obvious:** Jim’s story hints at terrors lying underground. The newspaper clipping adds to the tale of Sissy’s missing uncle, reinforcing the idea that someone or something is up to no good, out there in the countryside around Westhaven. The reference to sinkholes should also resonate when the scoutinvestigators reach **The Burrows** (page 76).
- After meeting Boxcar Jim, proceed to **Starting the Hike** (following).

### Starting the Hike

The scouts start in the parking lot for the trailhead to Orth-Beane Forest Preserve early on Saturday morning. So far, the weather looks quite pleasant, and no rain is due for the next few days. It is a little colder than normal, but not uncomfortably so; assistant scout leader Blackwell wears shorts, insisting the weather is _“Fresh.”_
The plan is to attempt to finish the first full hiking loop in one day. But the plan allows for an overnight stay, if necessary. The scouts’ rides return in the evening and wait from 7–9 pm. If they return after that time, they must camp at the trailhead until morning when the vehicles return.

### Dense Fog Advisory

The first 5 miles (8 km) pass uneventfully. The scouts can take note of wildlife amid water and snack breaks. As they are making excellent time, Blackwell allows for an extended stop for lunch, near a small spring-fed waterfall. Near mile 10 (kilometer 16), a mist settles on the forest floor and gets thicker with each forest section they pass; it soon becomes a dense fog. The sun is shrouded by the fog, giving the area a dusk-like quality.

Blackwell’s periodic stops to check the map become more frequent. He jollies everyone along, but his confidence clearly wanes. Soon, visibility is down to about 20 feet (6 m), and then 10 feet (3 m). Eventually, the scouts realize that they have completely lost their hiking path (no dice rolls needed). Blackwell’s enthusiasm is undiminished, _“Don’t worry, buddies. I’ll get you back or my name’s not Don Blackwell!”_ Attempts to use the sun to orient their position are useless. If a scout has a compass, it acts oddly. First, north points one way; then, after traveling a short distance, the compass shows north pointing a different way.

As the scouts gather their thoughts, they feel a vibration in the ground, as if from a minor earthquake. It lasts for 20 seconds, then slowly fades away.

- A successful **Geology** or Hard **EDU** roll reveals this area is known to be tectonically stable.

**Keeper note:** the ground tremors are caused by the burrowing assassin.

### _**leads**_

- **Obvious:** clearly, the earth tremors are an ill omen— astute scout-investigators might connect them to the sinkholes mentioned in the newspaper article (see **Handout: Tremors 1** ).
- Proceed to **The Burrows** (following).

### The Burrows

The scouts see a bizarre sight as they continue searching through the fog for the trail. As they survey the area, they find several strange holes in the ground. The holes are approximately 3–6 feet (1–2 m) in diameter.

- Ask anyone studying the holes for a **Spot Hidden** or **Track** roll: a success notes shallow trenches that look like drag marks between the holes.
- **Natural World** can determine that the holes have been dug from below, as if something were burrowing up. The holes look like enormous gopher holes or rabbit burrows. The scouts are not aware of any animal in this region that makes burrows this large.

Wishing to reassure the scout-investigators and raise group morale, Blackwell calls out. They turn to see him standing in one of the holes, a big smile on his face, _“It’s nothing to be frightened of buddies. See, it’s just a hole in the—aghhhh!”_ With that final scream, Blackwell disappears into the ground, terror on his face. By the time anyone gets to the hole, only Blackwell’s scout hat remains.

- Ask everyone to make a **Cool** roll—refer to **Cool** (page 43) for the results.
- If anyone chooses Flight after the Cool roll, they could tumble into a hole—ask for a **DEX** roll to avoid this; if failed, call for **CON** roll (see **Adversity: Feeling Sore** , page 46).

- Ask for a **Survival** roll from whomever has the highest skill: failure means no food—see **Adversity: Feeling Hungry** (page 45).

**Keeper note:** the burrowing assassin attacked and killed another forest creature on its nightly hunt.

### Someone’s Best Friend

Either during the first night or later the second day, the scouts hear something running through the woods.

- Ask for a **Listen** roll: whoever succeeds (or the one with the highest Listen skill) hears something coming toward them—and fast.

Build the tension here for the players, playing up the crack of twigs and an odd jingle of metal. Eventually, a flash of white and tan explodes from nearby bushes and toward the nearest scout—it’s a dog! The mixed breed mutt is exceptionally friendly and excited to see them. She goes up to each and sniffs while seeking attention. Obviously, she belongs to someone due to her collar, which bears a hand-engraved metal tag that says “Katy.”

She appears to be starving and readily devours any snacks the scouts give her (if they have any left!). If anyone is concealing food, Katy sniffs it out—this could provide some drama, especially if a scoutinvestigator is hiding some from the others.

- Katy accompanies the scouts on their journey, especially if they show her kindness. If they meet her on **The First Night** (page 77), she leads the scouts back to their camp and waits with them till morning.

### The Old Outhouse

In the north-east corner of the clearing stands an outhouse built from rough-hewn planks. The door is closed and has a hand-carved crescent moon cut into it. At first glance, the outhouse seems like it could accommodate just one person at a time, a true “one-holer.”

If the scouts open the door, they smell a foul stench (it is an outhouse after all). The ceiling is thick with cobwebs and some bloated spiders. The walls inside have carvings of animals, flowers, trees, and so on. Obviously, the owner had some time and a flair for art.

### The Trail

The trail winds several miles through the woods for before emerging on a dirt logging road.

To the north, the logging road goes 5 miles (8 km) to the north before dead-ending. Heading south, the road extends 3 miles (4. 8 km) and connects to another dirt road. Following the second dirt road in either direction eventually arrives, around 10 hours later, at houses and other civilized sights. So, following the trail back to safety takes around 10 hours. Depending on when they start out, it may necessitate a second night outdoors and provides the burrowing assassin a chance to attack (see **The Assassin Attacks**, page 85).

### Inside the Cabin

If the scouts peek through the dirty windows, the interior is dark. Tattered curtains obscure the view. Watching, the scouts can determine that there’s no movement inside, nor can they hear any sound within. Katy acts excited, pawing the front door like she wants to enter.

Inside, the only light comes from outside (depending on the time of day). The cabin has no electricity, but an old half-full oil lamp sits on a table; however, there’s no matches.

- A successful **Survival** roll enables a scout to create a small fire with which to light the oil lamp; this better illuminates the cabin.
- The oil lamp stands on a piece of paper, with a pencil nearby. When the scouts look at what’s on the paper, give them **Handout: Tremors 3** .
- Near the front door is a full gallon-sized metal can and an empty bucket. **Know** ( **EDU** ) may discern that the liquid is kerosene and flammable, but not explosively so.
- On a table lies an open book, next to an animal skull—see **The Kitchen Table** (page 82).

With some light, the old cabin’s poor condition becomes obvious, though it shows signs of recent occupation. Rot permeates the air throughout, but no source is evident.

The rest of the cabin is sparsely decorated. A chest of drawers containing pots and pans lines the south wall, with an earthenware bowl and pitcher sitting atop (no running water). Next to the bowl rests a butcher block. A small, cold wood stove in the corner serves for cooking and for heat. On top of the stove, a pot holds yesterday’s venison stew.

The bedroom door consists of a curtain strung across the doorway. Inside, there’s a bed and a double wardrobe. The bed remains unmade, and a few blankets lie on top. A pile of threadbare, holey clothes sits beside the bed.

- A successful **Spot Hidden** roll finds one box of matches and three shotgun shells.
- The main area has a closed, closet door with light, scratch marks. The smell of rot is stronger here— perhaps the source is behind it. See **Opening the Closet Door** (page 83).

### Starting the Countdown

As an option, use a countdown clock to determine when the burrowing assassin arrives. Start when one of the following occurs:

- The scout-investigator who received the dream message sees Norma Gross’ corpse (see **Whispers in the Dark** , page 78).
- The scout-investigators begin studying **Minter’s Manual** (page 83).

If Gross’ corpse triggers the countdown, inform the scout-investigator who received dream message that they receive a vivid mental message: the monster is coming!

- Ask that scout for a **Cool** roll; a failure means _“It’s coming!”_ spills involuntarily from the scoutinvestigator’s mouth for all to hear.

Otherwise, or alongside the new warning message, place one countdown token (coins, beads, etc.) on your table per player (minimum of two tokens). Next, ask everyone for a **Luck** roll: remove one token for every failure. Then, tell players that the tokens represent a countdown to the monster’s arrival. Use the tokens to create tension.

- Every time a scout-investigator comes to the end of focusing on the skull (see **Appendix C: Spells** , **Enchanting the Totem** , page 171), ask each player to make another **Luck** roll; reduce the tokens by one for each failure.
- If the scout-investigators settle down to sleep, or if they all leave the cabin, ask each player to make another **Luck** roll; reduce the tokens by one for each failure.

- If the scout-investigators set out along The Trail (page 80), or **Find Their Own Path** (page 86), after the first few miles, ask each player to make another **Luck** roll; reduce the tokens by one for each failure.

### Countdown: Half-time

When half the tokens have been removed, the ground shakes—foreshadowing the arrival of the burrowing assassin. They still have time to act, such as one last attempt at **Enchanting the Totem** (page 171), if they have been unsuccessful so far.


### Countdown: Time’s Up

When the final token is removed, the burrowing assassin arrives.

### Power of the Totem

The burrowing assassin cannot come within 10 feet (3 m) of the active totem. Scout-investigators remain safe so long as they stay in range. Once activated, the totem keeps the burrowing assassin at bay for one hour for each magic point invested. Ideally, scout-investigators invested at least 10 magic points in the totem. This affords 10 hours of safe passage, enough to head down the trail near the cabin (see **The Trail**, page 80; or they could try to **Find Their Own Path**, page 86) to reach civilization—see **The Aftermath**, page 87.

The burrowing assassin may rush toward an active totem, abruptly stopping just 10 feet (3 m) short of it.

- The first time this happens, all near the totem must each make a **Cool** roll.
- If the totem is being held by someone who fails their **Cool** roll, they drop the totem to the floor as an involuntary action.
- Should this happen, ask the scout holding the totem to make a **Luck** roll.
- **Failure:** an eye rolls out of the skull as it hits the floor, and the totem ceases to function.

It only takes one round and a successful **DEX** roll to replace the eye, making the totem active again. However, in that time, the burrowing assassin can attack.

### Option: Feeding Norma to the Monster

**Thomas Cline** (page 60) is sending mental images of Norma Gross to the burrowing assassin, instructing it to kill her. Neither Cline nor the burrowing assassin know that Gross is already dead.

- If the scout-investigators’ situation becomes desperate, ask for an **INT** roll (see **Leap of Logic** , page 47).
- **Success:** the scout-investigator gets an idea—in the scrawled note (see **Handout: Tremors 3** ), Gross said she believed that the thing was hunting for her. Perhaps if they fed her to the monster, it might leave?

If players come up with this idea on their own, all the better. Either way, it provides an alternative way of concluding the story.

### Find Their Own Path

Finding the way back to civilization is challenging. Compasses continue to behave erratically while the burrowing assassin roams the forest.

- Use a **Cool** roll to determine how scout-investigators deal with the stress of **Adversity: Being Lost** (page 45).
- After a few hours of observation, ask for a **Navigate** roll from the scout with the highest **Navigate** skill.
- **Success:** they determine which way the sun is moving and find the east-west line—guiding them home to safety.
- **Failure:** they may push the roll, otherwise they remain lost.
- **Fumble or failed pushed roll:** the ground begins to tremble, heralding the burrowing assassin’s attack.
