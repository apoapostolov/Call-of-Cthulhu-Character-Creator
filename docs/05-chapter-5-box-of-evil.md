### Keeper Information

Colonel Josef Grimm first encountered the secretive fraternal society, the **Sons of Seth** (page 60), at a grand dinner in Cairo, Egypt. He was accustomed to attending such affairs, due to his celebrity as a renowned adventurer and explorer. Grimm would enjoy his host’s hospitality in exchange for telling his outrageous stories.

Intrigued by the secret society, Grimm accepted their invitation. The Sons of Seth is an international organization, with new groups springing up around the globe.

When he had the opportunity to join the temple in his hometown of Westhaven he jumped, secretly aspiring to becoming its leader. So, Grimm held masked balls and gatherings, secretly courting scions of Westhaven’s wealthy families with the intention of recruiting them. While most view the sect’s secrets and ceremonies as quaint or thrilling, only a few know the real inner workings. Now in his 70s, Grimm yearns for a quieter life, but has no wish to retire.

Town council vice president, **Thomas Cline** (page 60), was the original founder of the Westhaven Temple of Seth. Cline became most aggrieved when Grimm took over and has nursed a grudge ever since.

Spotting an opportunity for revenge, Cline arranged for one of Grimm’s packages to be intercepted and replaced with a dangerous trap.

Grimm believes he is receiving a gemstone artifact that allows divination of the future. The package actually contains a wicker box, which hides a homunculus. When freed, the creature has been instructed to command the mummy on display in Grimm’s lounge to kill everyone in the house—starting with Grimm!

Cline plans to clean up any loose ends and retrieve the wicker box later. He knows authorities will conclude some sort of burglary occurred which ended in tragedy.

### Involving the Scout-investigators

One or more scout-investigators observes a fancy black car pull up to Colonel Grimm’s house. Two men in dark suits unload a moderately sized wooden crate from the back of the car. One appears to, strangely, say something to the crate, and then waves his hands over it in a weird manner. It’s all most curious. The two men take the crate inside Grimm’s house and exit several minutes later, empty-handed, and depart.

Everyone knows Colonel Grimm’s house is full of amazing and exotic artifacts. The idea of getting a peek at his latest prize should pique the scoutinvestigator’s interest. If desired, perhaps a scoutinvestigator could be related to Colonel Grimm.

- **Playing as a stand-alone scenario:** any scoutinvestigators can be used.
- **Playing as part two of the Westhaven Campaign:** two years have passed since **Tremors Below** (page 70), and it’s now 1920 (the exact date is unimportant); scout-investigators should now be **Rovers** (page 29).

### Colonel Grimm’s Mansion

Colonel Grimm’s mansion (# 17 on the **Westhaven Map**, page 54) is a stately, two-story colonial-style home located on a wealthy block near downtown Westhaven. The large plot boasts a beautiful garden, tended by hired staff. The property sits back off the main street with a jug-handle driveway; huge, evergreen arborvitae obscure most of the building from the street.

Grimm’s mansion is a shrine to the turn-of-thecentury concept of manliness embodied by the likes of Theodore Roosevelt, Sitting Bull, and Buffalo Bill Cody. Every hallway and room feature artifacts from Grimm’s journeys. These items range from strange to downright terrifying—nothing mundane comes into this house!

### Using Stealth

In essence, scout-investigators have two ways to creep into the mansion:

- Use **Stealth** to sneak in via an open door or window.
- Use **Mechanical Repair** or a Hard **STR** roll to break in.
- Create a distraction (or wait for one) and use that to dart in while the housekeeper is occupied.
- Or use a **Luck** roll to determine how quickly a “naturally occurring” distraction, such as the mail or other delivery, happens: success means sometime in the next hour, while failure means waiting for 1D4+1 hours.

Of course, if scout-investigators create their own distraction, no Luck roll is needed. However, Keepers may call for a **Charm** , **Fast Talk** , or **Persuade** roll from the person causing the distraction to ensure Mrs. Habersham remains occupied while their friends slip inside.

If caught, Mrs. Habersham expels the scoutinvestigators and sends them on their way.

**Note:** accessing the mansion is easy if one of the scoutinvestigators is related to Grimm, such as his great nephew or niece. Failing that, scout-investigators must rely on secrecy and stealth—see **Using Stealth** , following.

### What Happens Next?

The scenario assumes scout-investigators leave or get ejected after entering the study, with dismissal resulting from opening the box and releasing the homunculus. The scouts may then research information found in the study and, possibly, create the “Magical Powder” described in the journal.

Or, perhaps, having seen the homunculus, they decide to study that bizarre creature instead. This is fine, go with the flow; but on releasing the homunculus, the scoutinvestigators should, ideally, be thrown out for a time, to allow events inside the house to gather momentum.

If players aren’t interested in research, think of ways to delay them so that time passes before they return. Consider the scouts’ differing **Obligations** (page 28),  and what parents or guardians say and do if they are called by Grimm or Mrs. Habersham. Perhaps a **Trusted Adult** (page 25) asks them to perform a favor, like helping to carry groceries home, and so on.

However, if players refuse to leave the grounds, then play things out accordingly. They can watch events unfold through the windows and sneak back inside a short time later. As the homunculus ramps up the action, the players can jump in when they see fit. Be flexible and perhaps improvise a little. See **Return to Grimm’s** (page 103) for an overview of how the story should play out; feel free to adapt and change as necessary.

### Playing Out the Struggle

The struggle with the homunculus and mummy can play out in a variety of ways. Several options are considered here.

### _**back in Your Box!**_

- If scout-investigators attempt to herd the homunculus back into the wicker box, treat this as an **Assisted Fighting Maneuver** (page 38).
- If a scout is using **The Black Stone** (page 105), grant the group one bonus die for the maneuver.
- If the scout-investigators succeed in forcing the homunculus back into the wicker box, they must re-seal the box somehow—such as with twisted wire— while one works to keep it closed (e.g., sitting on top).

### _**trap an Enemy in a Room**_

- If a scout-investigator wishes to lock or bar a door, ask for a **Luck** roll:
- **Success:** if they are inside the room, they can bolt the door. Or, if they possess Mrs. Habersham’s keys, they can lock the door.
- **Failure:** the door has no lock or bolt.

### Breaking Through a Door

- Breaking through a door requires a Hard **STR** roll. Or a lock may be picked with **Locksmith** or Hard **Mechanical Repair** .
- If the mummy attempts to break through a door, ask for a group **Luck** roll each round; failure means the door splinters and crashes open.

### _**mummy Issues**_

The mummy moves slowly. It shuffles along with a stiff, halting gait; however, it does not tire and pursues the scouts with the intent of completing its orders of killing everyone in the house.

This may set up additional encounters as the mummy follows the scouts, while the homunculus tries to trip or bite them, throws objects, and so on. Scout-investigators may even try to set traps to capture or immobilize


their foes—reward ingenuity and decide which skill or characteristic roll can determine if their plan works. If they coordinate and enact the plan with skilled teamwork, consider awarding one bonus die.

### _**it’s a Wrap!**_

Scout-investigators may attempt to bind the mummy up in a bedsheet or similar.

- Treat this as an **Assisted Fighting Maneuver** (page 38). With success, the mummy is bound and unable to attack.
- Ask for a group **Luck** roll at the end of each subsequent round **:** failure sees the mummy tear its way free of the sheet.

### _**push Mummy Down the Stairs!**_

The mummy pursues the scout-investigators through the house. If upstairs, the scout-investigators can attempt to lure it to the top and push it down.

- Treat this as an **Assisted Fighting Maneuver** (page 38) and award one bonus die if the mummy is bound in a sheet already (see **It’s a Wrap** , preceding).
- Tumbling down the stairs causes the mummy 1D8 damage (the damage ignores armor) and renders it inactive for the following round.


### _**flames!**_

Scout-investigators may use the **Immolate** spell given to them by Boxcar Jim (see **Boxcar Jim’s Useful Items** , page 63). Or they could try to ignite it by a more traditional method—but that likely involves getting in range of its deadly grasp.

If the mummy is killed with fire, unless scoutinvestigators act immediately to extinguish the flames, they quickly spread to the soft furnishings; soon, the whole mansion is ablaze, ultimately burning to the ground. Almost all evidence gets destroyed by the fire; the only clues left are the charred remains of Colonel Grimm, Mrs. Habersham (if she didn’t escape), and the mummy.

### Fleeing the Mansion

When scout-investigators exit the mansion, they are surprised to discover the building has been transported into an endless desert. The sun beats down upon them with an intense, dry heat. Nothing but sand in any direction, except for one striking object. About 1 mile (1.6 km) away stands a large pyramid close to completion. One block remains to install, and workers are preparing to place it now.

**Keeper note:** the desert scene is an illusion, a magical hallucination created when the mummy was animated. Once the scouts exit the mansion, they become subject to the illusion.

- Using fire against the homunculus requires a successful **DEX** roll. The fire causes 1D6 points of damage, but it only burns for one round, then the flames die.
- Against the mummy, the flames automatically take hold (no roll). The fire causes 1D6 damage per round (ignore armor) until it runs out of hit points.
- If desired, the homunculus could try to extinguish the burning mummy by throwing a vase of water. The scouts can try to stop this with a successful **DEX** roll to catch it in midair. If they fail, call for a group **Luck** roll; failure extinguishes the fire!
- Witnessing this strange, out-of-place scene demands a **Cool** roll.
- A successful **INT** roll (see **Leap of Logic** , page 47) reveals they cannot return to their world while the mummy survives.
