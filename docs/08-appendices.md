### Appendix B: Glossary of Scouting Terms

**Lashings:** a specialized use of knots and wrapping of rope and twine. Typically used to secure logs together for creating camp gadgets, signal towers, etc.

**Semaphore:** using handheld flags to communicate at a distance; flags in various positions represent letters and words.

**Quartermaster:** this Wayfarer leadership position maintains the unit’s gear, checks it out for outings, and ensures it gets returned.

**Ranger:** the third rank in the Wayfarer Scouts. Must demonstrate advanced first aid; axe, saw, and knife safety; cooking; fire building; map and compass skills; and camping techniques to gain this rank.

**Rover:** the second rank in the Wayfarer Scouts. They have shown skill in basic first aid, camping, citizenship, hiking, knot-tying, and map and compass skills to advance from Wanderer.

**Scribe:** this Wayfarer squad leadership position makes notes and chronicles squad activities.

**Scout Leader:** while the Wayfarer Scouts strongly prefer youth members being their own leaders, adult supervision is important for guidance and support. The scout leader is the primary adult leader for each unit. A scout leader typically has several assistant scout leaders helping.


**Squad:** a Wayfarer Scout squad is the primary operating group within a unit. Each squad consists of four to eight mixed-gender scouts who work together to learn important outdoor skills and achieve rank advancement.

**Unit:** a Wayfarer Scout unit is a collection of squads typically bound together by geography, such as a local community. The unit is overseen by adult volunteers helping guide scouts on their journey through the organization.

**Wanderer:** the first rank in the Wayfarer Scouts. They are typically the youngest scouts in the organization.

**Warden:** the fourth and final rank in the Wayfarer Scouts. Once all Warden requirements, including the Warden service projects, are completed, the scout earns the title of Chief Warden.

**Wayfarer Scouts:** a fictional youth organization. The co-ed group emphasizes learning outdoor skills and improving leadership skills.

### Appendix C: Spells

**Note:** the Sanity costs of spells applies only when cast by NPCs; Sanity point cost is replaced by Luck point cost for scout-investigators.

### Chant of Thoth

- **Cost:** 10 magic points; 1D4 Sanity points (see note)
- **Casting time:** 30 minutes

### Body Warping of Gorgoroth

- Cost: 6 + magic points; 5 POW; 2D6 Sanity points (see note)

- **Casting time:** 1D6+4 minutes

Increases the caster’s mental faculties, boosting their chance of solving a particular intellectual problem.

The spell grants one bonus die for a roll to gain knowledge, learn a spell, translate a passage, deduce the meaning of a symbol, and so on.

Allows the caster to change physical form.

The sorcerer can change to any shape and appearance, but retains personal abilities. The form is fleshly, but can appear to be made of stone, wood, fabric, etc. Once changed into a new shape, the caster has the mobility of that shape. The caster’s STR, CON, INT, POW, and DEX do not change. If emulating a person, the sorcerer’s APP becomes that of the individual emulated. The caster can only take forms known to them.

First, the caster invokes Nyarlathotep. Then, they repeat the spell’s phrases while expending 6 magic points; spend 1 additional magic point per 5 SIZ points to be gained or lost. Only one alteration per casting is possible, and the effect is permanent until the spell is recast to change back again. This spell cannot be cast on another being.

### Cloud Memory

### Dominate

- Cost: 1 magic point; 1 Sanity point (see note)

- **Casting time:** instantaneous

Bends the will of the target to that of the caster. A successful opposed POW roll with the target means that they obey the caster’s commands without exception until the next round concludes.

The spell can affect only one individual at a time and has a maximum range of 10 yards/meters. Obviously, the command(s) must be intelligible to the target; the spell ends if a command contradicts the target’s basic nature (such as instructing a human to fly).

Dominate can be cast and recast as many times as the caster finds possible, allowing a target to be controlled without interruption for several minutes. Recasting has the same costs and limits. Recasting is instantaneous.

- **Cost:** 1D6 magic points; 1D2 Sanity points (see note)
- **Casting time:** instantaneous

### Dust of Suleiman

- Cost: 10 magic points per 3 doses

Blocks the target’s ability to consciously remember a particular event.

The caster must be able to see the target, and the target must be able to receive the caster’s instructions. If the caster wins an opposed POW roll with the target, the spell takes effect immediately. If successful, the target has one specified incident blocked from their memory. If the incident was terrifying, the target may thereafter still have nightmares vaguely relating to it. If the spell fails, the event in question becomes vivid in the target’s mind.

The caster must know the specific event to be blocked. They cannot command something vague such as _“Forget what you did yesterday.”_ Instead, they must cite a certain event, such as _“Forget you were assaulted by a monster.”_ This spell cannot block knowledge of spells or of the Cthulhu Mythos unless the knowledge is firmly tied to a specific event. It cannot undo Sanity loss or reverse the effects of Sanity loss.

- Casting time **:** 24+ hours (one round to apply one dose)

Creates a gray-green powder that harms unnatural beings from beyond this world.

The powder requires the dust from an Egyptian mummy at least 2, 000 years old; each mummy suffices for 3 doses of the magic dust. Additional ingredients include frankincense, saltpeter, and sulfur. An incantation must be said over the dust while being sprinkled. A being not of this Earth loses 1D20 hit points per dose. It takes one round to apply a dose.

The powder works only on extraterrestrial and other planar beings; thus, it would harm a dimensional shambler, Yog-Sothoth, a mi-go, or the homunculus, but is useless against a shoggoth or a deep one.


### Enthrall Victim

- **Cost:** 2 magic points; 1D6 Sanity points (see note)
- **Casting time:** 1 round

The caster must be able to speak calmly with the target before the spell can take effect. After a round or so of talking, make an opposed POW roll between the caster and target. If the caster succeeds, the target stands dumbstruck and numb until relieved from the trance by physical assault or some similarly shocking event.

### Evil Eye

- **Cost:** 10 magic points; 1D4 Sanity points (see note)
- **Casting time:** 1 round

### Enchanting the Totem (new Spell)

- **Cost:** 10 magic points, plus those invested
- **Casting time:** 1D4 hours preparation of skull, 1 hour to cast

Creates a totem that briefly wards off some creatures. The caster acquires a deer skull and inscribes it with arcane sigils. The hollow eye sockets are filled with clay and then smoothed to resemble the animal’s eyes. The caster stares into the clay eyes while repeating a series of seemingly nonsense words.

The caster then makes a Cool (or Sanity, if regular _Call of Cthulhu_ ) roll: if successful, the clay eyes change into those of a living deer (only the caster sees this), and the caster can invest 1D10 magic points into the skull. Repeated castings may increase the number of magic points invested. With magic points invested, the totem is empowered, and the eyes transform into glowing orbs, which anyone can see.

When empowered, the totem deters burrowing assassins (and possibly other Mythos monsters), which cannot come within 10 feet (3 m) of the totem. The effect lasts for 1 hour per magic point invested.

**Note:** scout-investigators must make a Cool roll to invest magic points in the totem. Each can take a turn to do so, and, if failed, can be retried:

- **Success:** the scout-investigator keeps their Cool; over the next hour, they lose 1D10 magic points, which become invested in the totem.
- **Failure:** the scout-investigator loses their Cool (ask them how); they instantly lose 1D6 magic points which do not empower the totem, but nothing else happens.

Causes a target to suffer from bad luck. The target must be within sight of the caster. They will not necessarily sense the Evil Eye’s influence, but may feel an odd chill or unease when the spell is first cast.

A successful casting halves the target’s chance for a Luck roll.* The target must apply one penalty die to all characteristic and skill rolls, their guns jam on rolls of 75% or higher, melee weapons break on rolls of 75% or higher, and the chance to successfully cast a Summon/Bind spell is halved.

These effects continue until next sunrise, the caster removes the spell, the caster is found and blooded (hit hard enough to bleed), or the target dies.

* _Neither can the target spend or gain Luck points._

### Reanimation

- Cost: 6 magic points; 5 POW; 3D6 Sanity points (see note)

- **Casting time:** 6 hours

Not to be confused with Dr. Herbert West’s scientific process of reanimation; this spell creates a form of self-aware zombie under its creator’s control.

The entity retains all knowledge and skills that it possessed in life. This ghastly spell requires a recently dead human corpse that retains enough flesh to allow mobility upon activation. The caster must know the deceased’s name. The ritual includes an elaborate incantation chanted while the caster washes the corpse with their tongue.

Should the body suffer injury, like the severing of an arm or head, it can be sewn back on without undue impediment. The death of the caster frees the reanimated corpse from servitude; however, unlike a zombie, the reanimated corpse continues to function after its creator’s demise as an independent entity.

### Summon Burrowing Assassin (new Spell)

- **Cost:** variable magic points; 1D4 Sanity points (see note)
- **Casting time:** 5 minutes per magic point spent

Summons a deadly creature from below. The sacrifice of each magic point provides 10 percentiles chance for success in summoning a burrowing assassin (e.g., 3 magic points give a 30% chance of success). A result of 96–100 always fails—and 100 should always have adverse consequences for the caster. The caster also loses 1D4 Sanity points per casting, regardless of result.

If a success, one burrowing assassin appears per spell, 2D10 game minutes after the chant concludes. The Keeper decides whether the burrowing assassin arrives bound. If it does not, the Keeper may ask the caster to make an opposed POW roll versus the burrowing assassin. If successful, the burrowing assassin arrives bound; if failed, the assassin attacks the caster, and then likely returns whence it came.

If bound, the burrowing assassin must obey one order by the caster, including attacking its own kind, after which it is freed.

### Tongue of the Woods (new Spell)

Creates a long, viny tongue in the victim’s mouth, which acts as a weapon against them.

The caster must be able to see the target. When cast, a thick vine begins to grow inside the target’s mouth, extending like some awful tongue to slowly wrap around the target’s head. If the caster succeeds with an opposed POW roll versus the target, the vine grows and starts to choke the victim, inflicting 1D3 damage each round.

The vine can be pulled from the victim’s throat with a successful STR roll; however, the victim must succeed with a CON roll or suffer 1D3 damage from the violent removal.

**Deeper Magic:** if the target is dead and skinned, the caster may invest a further 10 magic points; this causes the vines to grow from the corpse, wrapping around its torso and limbs and adhering to exposed muscle. Growth continues until the corpse is completely wrapped in a thick, woven exoskeleton of ivy and branches. The corpse is finally prepared by replacing its heart (which the caster consumes) with a fetish made of a knot of twine wrapped around a smaller heart, that of a bird or rodent. Once accomplished, the corpse can be animated to become a **Wicker One** (see page 162).

- **Cost:** 4 magic points; 1D6 Sanity points  (see note)
- **Casting Time:** 1 round


### Appendix D: Quick Reference

### Adversity Checklist

**Cold:** Survival roll. **Hunger:** Survival roll. **Lost:** Cool roll. **Overburdened:** modify MOV rate (–1 or –3). **Sore:** CON roll. **Three checked:** penalty die on Cool rolls. **All five checked:** two penalty dice on Cool rolls.

### Cool

Cool replaces Sanity. If failed, player chooses one of:

- Successful Reassure roll (once per scenario).
- Scout-Investigator Development Phase.

### Cumbersome Weapons

Make a **STR** roll:

- **Success:** use weapon without penalty.
- **Failure:** roll Fighting attack roll with a penalty die.

### Leap of Logic

Make an **INT** roll: Keeper provides a clue or hint.

- Fight
- Flight
- Freeze
- Fawn
- Flop

### Maneuver Checklist

1. Decide goal.

2. Decide who takes the lead.

3. Determine number assisting.

4. Compare lead’s modified build with opponent.

5. Determine if lead gains bonus or penalty dice.

And check one Distress box (Stressed, Jumpy, Upset) ( **Optional:** involuntary action)

6. Make rolls to determine outcome.

### Wounds & Healing Checklist

**Recovery:** erase one Distress check when any of the following occur:

- Spends night safe at home.
- Spends time with squad telling stories.

Healing: + 2 HP per day. **First Aid/Medicine:** +1D4 HP. **Major Wounds:** ignore. **Zero HP:** unconscious. **Dying:** single wound equal/greater than max HP. **Cheating Death:** spend all Luck (min. 30 points).

### Appendix E: Pre-generated Scout-investigators

Note: all eight scout-investigators are 11 -year-old Wanderers. If you wish to begin older, modify each using the progression modifiers in the **Scout-Investigator Development Phase** (page 47). All have Luck 60 as default, but feel free to replace this with a random roll (2D6+ 6, multiply by 5) if desired.

Additionally, we advise Keepers to grant each player 30 bonus points to spend on skills—increasing those listed or investing in a new skill. If the latter, add the bonus points to the skills “base value” written in parentheses next to the skill on the scout-investigator sheet. Note that any skill bonuses for the ability badges they hold have already been factored in.

### Author Biographies

**Joshua Hoyt** is the main creative for Black Rune Studio. Don’t try to find it—it hides in the darkness—where it belongs. He has published several adventures over the years and written countless convention games. This includes _Edge of the Endless Sea_ (5e) and the _Four Towers of Terror Trilogy_ (5e), along with this book, _Campfire Tales_ for _Call of Cthulhu_ .

Josh helps organize one of the coolest tabletop game conventions around, Gamehole Con. He is an avid tabletop gamer, comic book collector, and all-around fantasy nerd. He lives in Wisconsin. Don’t try to find him—he hides in the darkness—where he belongs.

**Paul Fricker** serves as an Associate Editor for _Call of Cthulhu_ . In partnership with Mike Mason, Paul is best known for writing and revising the latest edition of Chaosium’s award-winning RPG, _Call of Cthulhu_ . Over the years, he has participated in creating and developing numerous scenarios, beginning with _Gatsby and the Great Race_ (2005). More recently, Paul worked on the _Rivers of London Roleplaying Game_ for Chaosium.

Paul also co-hosts _Mason & Fricker’s Eldritch Stories_ , as well as the horror and gaming podcast, _The Good Friends of Jackson Elias_ . Find out the latest about Paul on his blog, “The Frickerverse” (paulfricker. substack.com), where he focuses on the important topics in life: gaming, music, and food!

**Matt Ryan** hails from the hills and hollows of Central New York. Matt began playing RPGs in the 1970s. But it was in 2004 when he made his first published contribution to the hobby with three adventures in _Living Lore_ for Atlas Games’ _Ars Magica_ , and became a regular contributor, authoring scenarios, supplements, and drawing maps.

In 2018, he drew maps for Chaosium’s _The Glorantha Sourcebook_ and the _Call of  Cthulhu_ scenario “Scritch Scratch.” From that point, Matt has devoted his creative energy to Chaosium cartography, with the odd convention scenario thrown in here and there. His work can be found throughout Chaosium’s books. Matt regularly attends Chaosium’s North American TTRPG conventions and always loves a chat.
